\begin{letter}{The Editors \\ \emph{Expert Systems with Applications}}

\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please find enclosed our manuscript, \textbf{``Pairwise Judging versus Voting for Language Model Answer Selection: Finite-Pool Theory and an Empirical Audit,''} submitted as a research article to \emph{Expert Systems with Applications}.

\textbf{Problem and relevance.} Intelligent systems built on language models often sample several answers and must return one. The designer chooses between a free rule, the most frequent answer (self-consistency voting), and a paid component, a pairwise judge. The paper gives a decision rule for that choice and tests it on stored runs. Related applied work in this journal uses language models inside decision systems, for example for group consensus decision-making and for maintenance decisions; our question is complementary: how to select among sampled answers and when a judge call is worth its cost.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item An exact finite-pool break-even judge accuracy for binary Copeland selection against plurality voting (80.35\% at 80\% candidate accuracy for three candidates).
\item A result for problems of differing difficulty: the break-even is governed by an effective-odds ratio of difficulty moments, not pooled accuracy; per-problem prediction of the observed gap is checked on stored judgments.
\item A proof that voting first and judging only on ties is Bayes-optimal in the model and never worse than either rule, so it needs no accuracy threshold; on stored runs it uses only 4--7\% of the judge comparisons of judging everywhere and removes the loss that judging everywhere causes in heterogeneous pools.
\item A ground-truth audit of one free-tier judge on six public benchmarks (532 problems) with paired exact tests, stored outputs for offline replay and an exact Kemeny--Young reference.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Scope of the evidence.} The empirical findings concern the tested judge and configuration, not stronger judges: the judge shows no detected advantage over self-consistency in homogeneous pools, and vote-first selection adds no detectable accuracy over voting because exact vote ties are rare. Datasets are public (GSM8K, MATH Level 5, AIME 2025 and 2026, MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond, RewardBench). Code, public stored runs and tables are archived on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24112045).

No part of this work has been published, and the manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.
