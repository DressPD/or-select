\begin{letter}{The Editors \\ \emph{Expert Systems with Applications}}

\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please find enclosed our manuscript, \textbf{``Vote First, Judge on Ties: Cost-Aware Answer Selection for Language-Model Systems,''} submitted as an original research article. The manuscript and its supplement are anonymized for double anonymized review; the author details, acknowledgments and declarations are on the separate title page.

\textbf{Fit with the journal.} The journal publishes work on the design, development, testing and management of intelligent systems, with practical guidelines for builders. Language-model systems such as question-answering assistants and decision-support tools routinely sample several answers and must return one. The component that selects the answer is a design decision with a price: the most frequent answer (self-consistency voting) is free, a pairwise judge is paid and unreliable, and builders lack guidance on when the judge is worth calling. Related applied work in this journal uses language models inside decision systems, for group consensus decision-making and for maintenance decisions; our question is complementary and concerns the selection component itself.

\textbf{Main findings.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{A design rule.} Voting first and consulting the judge only on tied answers is Bayes-optimal in a finite-pool model and never worse than voting or than judging everywhere, so it needs no judge-accuracy threshold. On stored runs it uses 4 to 7\% of the judge comparisons that judging every problem needs and removes the loss that judging everywhere causes in heterogeneous pools.
\item \emph{When judging everywhere pays.} An exact break-even judge accuracy (80.35\% at 80\% candidate accuracy for three candidates), and for problems of differing difficulty a break-even set by an effective-odds ratio of difficulty moments, not by pooled accuracy.
\item \emph{An audit protocol and evidence.} One free-tier judge on six public benchmarks (532 problems) with paired exact tests on frozen candidate pools: no detected advantage over self-consistency in homogeneous pools, and no detectable gain from vote-first selection because exact ties are rare. The findings concern the tested judge, not stronger judges.
\item \emph{Design guidelines} for system builders, distilled from the results.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Data and reproducibility.} All datasets are public (GSM8K, MATH Level 5, AIME 2025 and 2026, MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond, RewardBench). Code, stored runs and tables are deposited in a public repository whose persistent identifier is given in the submission form; the identifier is withheld from the anonymized files.

No part of this work has been published, and the manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.
