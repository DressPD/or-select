\begin{letter}{The Editors \\ \emph{IISE Transactions}}

\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please find enclosed our manuscript, \textbf{``Pairwise Judging versus Voting for Language Model Answer Selection: Finite-Pool Theory and an Empirical Audit,''} for consideration in \emph{IISE Transactions}. The manuscript is anonymized for double-blind review; author details are on the separate title page.

Sampling several answers from a language model and returning one is a data-driven decision problem with a cost: take the most frequent answer, or pay for a pairwise judge. The paper contributes (i) an exact finite-pool break-even judge accuracy for binary Copeland selection against plurality voting; (ii) a result for problems of differing difficulty: the break-even is governed by an effective-odds ratio of difficulty moments, not pooled accuracy, and the per-problem prediction is checked on stored judgments; (iii) a proof that voting first and judging only on ties is Bayes-optimal in the model and never worse than either rule, so it has no break-even threshold; and (iv) a ground-truth audit of one free-tier judge on six public benchmarks (532 problems) with paired exact tests, stored outputs for offline replay, and an exact Kemeny--Young reference.

The empirical findings are deliberately narrow: the tested judge shows no detected advantage over self-consistency in homogeneous pools and performs worse when judging everywhere in heterogeneous pools; vote-first selection removes that loss but adds no detectable gain over voting because exact vote ties are rare. They concern the tested judge and configuration, not stronger judges. Code, public stored runs and tables are archived on Zenodo, with data-access conditions described in the manuscript.

No part of this work has been published, and the manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.

\closing{Sincerely,}
