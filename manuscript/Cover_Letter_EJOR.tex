\opening{Dear Professor S{\l}owi{\'n}ski,}

Please find enclosed our manuscript, \textbf{``Pairwise Judging versus Voting for Language Model Answer Selection: Finite-Pool Theory and an Empirical Audit,''} for consideration as a Theory and Methodology paper in the \emph{European Journal of Operational Research}.

The paper derives an exact accuracy comparison between binary Copeland selection and plurality voting for three independently generated candidates, with finite-pool extensions. It combines this result with a composite answer selector, an exact integerized Kemeny--Young reference and a stored-output ground-truth audit. The theoretical contribution quantifies the comparison reliability needed to improve answer correctness; the ranking reference evaluates agreement with the judge's preferences.

On six benchmarks (532 distinct problems), the tested free-tier judge configuration has no statistically detected homogeneous-pool advantage over voting and lower pooled heterogeneous-pool accuracy. Offline ablations identify an objective-scale mismatch and compare alternative selectors on the same saved comparisons. The Theory and Methodology contribution is the finite-pool analysis together with a decision-analytic audit separating ranking fit, objective design and correctness. Code, public stored runs and tables are archived on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24112045), with data-access conditions described in the manuscript.
